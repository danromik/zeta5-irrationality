\begin{abstract}
The project compiles. There is one theorem, \lean{Zeta5.zeta5\_irrational :
Irrational zeta5}, and the paper's entire route to it --- Theorem~2.1, Propositions~2.2,
4.1, 4.3, 5.1, 5.2, 6.3, Lemma~3.3 and the numbered equations between them --- is wired up
beneath it with nothing short-circuited. It rests on \emph{two} external axioms
(Hermite's integral formula for the Hurwitz zeta function, and the prime number theorem in
the partial-summation form the proof consumes; Mathlib has neither) and on \emph{one} named
\lean{sorry}: the paper's (6.14), the logarithmic-energy bound of \S6. The four other large
steps --- Lemma~3.3 at the entries of (3.11), the inner-range local analysis behind
Proposition~4.1, the outer-range local analysis (4.10)--(4.12), and the uniformity statement
(5.7) with its \S5.2 companions --- are \emph{proved}, with statements fixed before the proofs
were written and no further axiom. Every arithmetic statement that the proof of Theorem~1.1 uses
(\S\S2--5 and Appendix~B) is therefore machine-checked. At three places the formal proof takes a
different route from the paper's own proof: Lemma~3.2 (the distribution formula, with the far
poles), the polynomial part of the proof of Lemma~3.3, and the unimodularity arguments are
replaced by different arguments, so the paper's proofs of those three steps are bypassed rather
than checked. The assumption list is recomputed from the Lean environment on
every build and independently confirmed by a separate scan; \S6 records the faithfulness
audit against the PDF, including the places where the formal proof takes a different route
from the paper's.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{The ground rules}

Anything internal to Fauzan's argument is proved or a \lean{sorry}, never an axiom. Neither
axiom mentions any constant or table of the paper, and the only object of the paper that
either one mentions is the explicit weight $w$ of (2.10). A finite explicit computation is
never an axiom: Appendix~B's exact rational integrals and Appendix~A's Table-1 evaluations and
Table-2 tiling are \emph{proved}. And no statement is changed to make it provable: the
statements of the interface theorems were fixed before their proofs were written, and a
comparison at the level of kernel terms with a snapshot taken before the last of them were
proved shows that no statement or definition changed (\lean{docs/CERTIFICATION.md}, \S5).

\section{(B) The two external axioms}

\lean{Zeta5/Axioms.lean} is the only file containing \lean{axiom} declarations, and it
contains two. Each docstring gives the statement, a citation, and why Mathlib lacks it.

\subsection*{\lean{hermite\_pole\_integral}}
\[
  \int_0^\infty \frac{w(y)}{y^2+a^2}\,\mathrm{d}y \;=\; a^4\zeta(5,a) - \frac{1}{2a} - \frac14
  \qquad (a>0),
\]
with $w$ the weight of (2.10) and $\zeta(5,a)=\sum_{k\ge0}(k+a)^{-5}$: the display in the
middle of p.~5. \textbf{Citation:} DLMF 25.11.29, Hermite's integral formula for the Hurwitz
zeta function --- the reference the paper itself gives. \textbf{Why not Mathlib:} Mathlib has
\lean{HurwitzZeta.hurwitzZeta} and its functional equation, but no Hermite/Abel--Plana
integral representation and no theory of the Eisenstein-type weight $w$. It is slightly
stronger than DLMF 25.11.29 alone: the paper's four integrations by parts on p.~5 are absorbed
into it. Numerically confirmed from the Lean definition of $w$ at 40 digits.

\subsection*{\lean{pnt\_prime\_riemann\_sum}}
For $0\le a<b$ and $\varphi$ bounded on $[a,b]$ and continuous off a finite set,
\[
  \frac1X\sum_{\substack{p\ \mathrm{prime}\\ aX<p\le bX}}\varphi(p/X)\log p
  \;\longrightarrow\; \int_a^b\varphi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y \qquad (X\to\infty).
\]
\textbf{Citation:} the prime number theorem in the form $\theta(x)\sim x$ (the paper's
[9, \S27.12]), carried through Abel summation and the Darboux criterion (Apostol,
\emph{Introduction to Analytic Number Theory}, Thms 4.2, 4.4, \S4.3). \textbf{Why not
Mathlib:} as of v4.34.0 Mathlib has Chebyshev-type bounds but no $\theta(x)\sim x$.
It is the \emph{partial-summation corollary} of the PNT, the one place where a reader must
grant more than a bare citation; its hypotheses carry no information about the paper's
functions, and all of them are discharged inside \lean{PrimeSum.lean}. The certification
derives from it in Lean, with no \lean{sorry}, the dyadic form $(\theta(2X)-\theta(X))/X\to1$ of
the prime number theorem, so it is not vacuous.

\section{How the four large interface statements were proved}

All four are closed in the strict sense: \lean{\#print axioms} on each shows
no \lean{sorryAx} (verbatim: \lean{'Zeta5.outer\_local\_analysis' depends on axioms: [propext,
Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}, and likewise for the other three). Nothing was moved one
level down.

\textbf{Consequences.} Propositions 4.1, 4.3, 5.1, 5.2 and (3.12) are fully proved (5.2
modulo the PNT axiom). The \S4.1 combinatorics (\lean{Section41.lean}, including the repair of
the gap in the p.~10 inequality, item 18 below) and the \S4.2 local lemmas
(\lean{OuterLocal.lean}) are load-bearing: 104/148 and 98/107 of their declarations are in the
dependency cone of the main theorem.

\section{Faithfulness}

\textbf{Statements.} The statements of the four interface theorems proved last, of
\lean{eq\_6\_14} and of \lean{zeta5\_irrational} are byte-identical to those recorded in a
snapshot taken before the four proofs were written, and a comparison at the level of kernel
terms shows that no statement or definition of that snapshot changed
(\lean{docs/CERTIFICATION.md}, \S5).

\textbf{Statements checked against the PDF page images} (pp.~5--15, 18--20).
\lean{Lemma33.lemma\_3\_3} is Lemma~3.3 as printed on p.~8, for every $p$, $K$, $d$; its
hypothesis ``$A$ integer-valued on $\mathbb{Z}_p$'' is taken as $v_p(A(z))\ge0$ for
$z\in\mathbb{Z}$, which is equivalent by density. \lean{pullback} is (3.1) on the whole domain
of $\mu_X$. \lean{raabe\_tau} is (3.7) for polynomials. The weights of \lean{OuterBasis} are
the doubled (4.12) exactly, zero class included. \textbf{No weakened statement was found.}

\medskip\noindent Deviations, all recorded in docstrings:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \lean{eq\_6\_14} is stated with $I(\rho)$ and $M_0$ as \emph{defined real numbers} ---
  the closed form (A.2) and $-1329/200$ --- not as the logarithmic energy. Do not read
  ``(6.4) is proved'' as ``Lemma 6.1 is proved''.
\item \lean{lemma\_3\_1} proves (3.4) for every \emph{partial} sum $\sum_{j\le J}p^jU_j$, not
  for the series in the Tate algebra. It is also \emph{not in the cone}: Proposition~4.1
  goes around it (item 7). The same holds for \lean{lemma\_4\_2} in its abstract-field form;
  Proposition~4.3 uses the rank form \lean{vanishing\_of\_rank}.
\item \textbf{\lean{outer\_local\_analysis} states the literal rank form of (4.10)},
  $\operatorname{rank}L\le r_p$. An earlier formulation of this interface statement, as a set
  of vanishing columns of $L$ in the basis (4.11), was false for the paper's own $L$ (found in
  the referee audit of the preprint) and was replaced before the proof was written. The proof
  shows why: the vanishing is in the monomial basis, exactly as p.~12 argues, and
  $L=UL_0U^{\mathsf T}$ carries the rank bound over. The statement proved is (4.10) as
  printed.
\item \lean{AppendixB.eq\_5\_16\_5\_17} proves the finite-interval difference form rather than
  the two improper integrals (5.16) and (5.17) --- formally weaker than p.~16, and exactly what
  is consumed.
\item \textbf{Lemma~3.3 is proved by a different route.} The polynomial part does not use the
  paper's two-scale estimate $P(\mathbb{Z}_p)\subset p^{-L_0-M_0}\mathbb{Z}_p$, (3.8) or (3.9);
  it expands $A$ in the binomial basis $\binom{x+K}{k}$ and computes $\tau$ of each quotient
  exactly. The \emph{statement} of Lemma~3.3 is verified; its printed proof is not checked line
  by line. The $-v_p(24)$ turns out to be unnecessary.
\item \lean{eq\_5\_7\_uniformity} bundles three claims with one constant, proved with
  $C=400M^2$.
\item \textbf{Lemma~3.2 (3.7) is not formalised as stated.} The Tate-algebra extension, (3.5),
  the parameter $Y=p^5X+C_p$, and (3.7) for rational functions do not appear. Proposition~4.1
  is proved without them: pole terms bounded directly, the polynomial part through Raabe
  (the polynomial half of (3.7), the paper's own justification) and a truncated-inverse
  estimate. Do not say ``Lemma~3.2 is formalised''.
\item $b_a<6\alpha x+3$ strictly, where the paper prints $\le$ (towards strength; see item 18).
\item Lemma~4.2 takes $v(\mathit{pinv})\ge-1$ rather than literally $p^{-1}$ (more general).
\item (5.21) and (5.16)--(5.18) are stated for $M\in40\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, as printed.
\item (5.8)/(5.9) are nested \lean{if}s, differing from the paper on a null set.
\item Naming trap: \lean{InnerAlloc.L 0} is not the paper's $L_0=4M+10$.
\item (6.11) is proved as printed via a sharper intermediate bound.
\item \lean{PrimeSum.RR\_bddOn} proves the crude $|R|\le200M^2$, not the paper's sharp bound;
  nothing needs more.
\item \textbf{The p.~12 divided-difference step needs more than the paper says.}
  $H^{(5)}_{p-a}\equiv H^{(5)}_{a-1}\pmod p$ alone leaves a residue $-1/a$ (machine-checked);
  the proof also uses the $-1/4+1/(2j)$ tail of (2.3), and the must-fail control shows it is
  essential. A point the author may want to revise, not an error in the result.
\item Unimodularity of (4.5) and (4.11) is proved by one mod-$p$ Hermite-basis argument
  rather than the paper's two separate justifications.
\item The \S4.2 local analysis uses only $p\ge7$, $K<3p$, $2K<p^2$, $2N<p$, $5N\le2p-2$ ---
  neither $p\le K$ nor $K\ge200M^2$ (towards strength).
\item \textbf{A gap on p.~10, repaired.} The paper asserts $b_a\le6\alpha x+3$ without proof,
  attributing it to (4.4); it is a statement about $\ell_N$, equivalent to
  $p\,\ell_N(a)\le2N+p$. The naive bound $\ell_A(a)\le2\lfloor A/p\rfloor+2$ gives only
  $b_a\le6\alpha x+6$, which does not make the dimensions of (4.4) provably nonnegative. The
  inequality is proved in the strict form $p\,\ell_A(a)<2A+p$ by the case split on
  $v_A=A\bmod p$ (\lean{Zeta5.ell\_lt\_caseSplit}) and independently from the closed form of
  $\ell_A$ (\lean{Zeta5.ell\_lt}, the one in the dependency cone); Lean checks by \lean{rfl}
  that the two statements are identical. A point the author may want to revise, not an error
  in the result.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Trusting the audit metaprogram}

\lean{Zeta5/Audit.lean} walks the dependency cone and reports the groups above. It avoids two
traps that a naive dependency walker falls into (\lean{ConstantInfo.value?} is \lean{none} for
theorems in Lean~4.34; reachability must match \lean{Lean.CollectAxioms.collect}), checks
itself against \lean{Lean.collectAxioms} on every build, and checks that no \lean{sorry}
escapes the cone. Its \lean{\#sorry\_tree} controls for the four interface statements of the
previous section, the shared lemma and seven of the main new theorems all print
\lean{depends on NO sorry}, and \lean{eq\_6\_14} prints \lean{rests on 1 sorry(s)}. An
adversarial certification (\lean{docs/CERTIFICATION.md}; scripts in \lean{cert/}, sharing no
code with \lean{Audit.lean}) agrees: 62\,097 constants in the cone, one \lean{sorry}, two
project axioms, zero orphans, a full kernel replay of every module by \lean{leanchecker}, and
the deliberately broken \lean{value?}-walker reporting the wrong answer (0 sorries), as a
negative control should.
